Why this exists

This isn't a hypothetical. It's built from stories like this one.

On August 23, 2026, two 20-year-old mothers — Iyanna Warr and Trinity Durham — were shot and killed at Family Scholar House, a student-housing community near the University of Louisville. Both were building a future for their kids. Neither had a way to instantly call the neighbors around them for help.

Stories like theirs repeat across the country every year, and they share a pattern: the person was alone, unseen, and unreachable in the moment it mattered most. The Raptor's Call exists to break that pattern — before violence happens, not just after.

Three tools, one network

Each piece works alone. Together, they turn a group of neighbors into a real-time safety net.

The Call

An alarm disguised as jewelry

A wearable tag that looks like an everyday accessory. Pull the pin, or press the hidden button, and it broadcasts your GPS location to every neighbor on your team instantly — no phone required, no cell signal needed.

The Raptor's Key

Turns your phone into a command center

A small dongle that plugs into any Android phone and puts your whole team on one live map. See where everyone is, send messages that never touch the internet, and get alerts even while the app runs quietly in the background.

The Grid

Sensors that watch the ground you can't

Solar-powered nodes that sense motion, heat, footsteps, and tripwires, and a pocket radar that can detect a person through walls or in total darkness from 30+ feet away. Add nodes over time to widen the net block by block.

It's watching your back, too

The Raptor's Call quietly notices when something's off — an unexpected route, a missed check-in, a sudden change in movement — and it doesn't wait for permission to act if it needs to.

  • If it detects a real risk and can't confirm you're okay, it can sound the alarm on your behalf — before you ever press anything.
  • If you lose signal in a basement, tunnel, or elevator, the network remembers your last known location and keeps retrying until a neighbor acknowledges it.

You decide who sees you — until it matters

Your icon on the team map follows whichever setting you choose. Threat detection and alarms keep running underneath, no matter what.

Always visible

Your team sees your real location at all times. Built for members actively on watch.

Hidden near home

Your icon disappears automatically within a radius you set around your home, and reappears everywhere else.

Ghost mode

Always hidden from the map. Built for neighbors who carry a higher personal risk.

Minor accounts

Anyone under 18 is automatically locked to ghost mode — no exceptions, no opt-out.

One rule never bends.

The instant it matters, everyone becomes visible — whether you pull your own alarm, a teammate raises it for you because they see you in danger, or the network itself flags a serious threat. Hidden is a setting for calm moments, never for emergencies.

Only vetted neighbors get in

Anyone can be watched over. Not anyone gets to watch.

  • Every applicant is checked through references, a personal interview, and a background check before they can see anyone's location.
  • New members start on probation with limited visibility until they've proven reliable.
  • Unusual behavior is flagged automatically, and repeat problems mean quiet, permanent removal — no warning given.
